The woman behind the welcome

Mariana grew up in Mexico City surrounded by women who understood that hospitality is not a service — it's a language. Her grandmothers Cecilia, María Elvira, Isabel, and Silvia each had their own way of making people feel at home: a perfectly set table, a bed prepared with care, flowers from the garden, coffee ready before you woke up.

After years in design, she realized what she really wanted to build was not a space, but a feeling — the one she had as a child arriving at her grandmother's house. That door opening. That smell of something cooking. That sense that someone had been thinking about you before you arrived.

Casa Caravana is the result. Four residences in a classic Mexican casa in the Anzures neighborhood, each carrying the name and personality of one of those grandmothers. Not as a tribute on a wall, but as a living philosophy in every detail.

“I don't want my guests to feel like they booked a place. I want them to feel like they were expected.”

What Mariana personally takes care of

  • 48 hours before your arrival: WiFi credentials, building access code, parking details, and her personal phone number — everything you need, no surprises
  • A curated neighborhood guide on your nightstand — her favorite restaurants, cafés, markets, and hidden corners of Anzures and Polanco
  • Hotel-quality linens, selected by hand. She notices thread count the way some people notice wine
  • Fresh coffee in your kitchen. Local, roasted in small batches, ready for your first morning
  • Plants in every corner — because her grandmothers taught her that a home without green things isn't really alive
  • Baby kit (crib, bath, high chair) prepared before you arrive — just ask in advance
  • Restaurant reservations, massage appointments, market tours, private transport — she connects you with her trusted local network
  • A personal message after check-in to make sure everything is perfect — and she means it
